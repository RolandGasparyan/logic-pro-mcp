# Movie Editor — Reference Tables v1.0

Companion to `SKILL.md`. All timecodes are examples; always measure against the cut.

## CUT TYPES

| Cut | Description | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Hard Cut | Direct transition | 95% of cuts |
| Match Cut | Visual / audio match | Smooth transition |
| Jump Cut | Same shot, time skip | Energy, urgency |
| Cutaway | Cut to related shot | Information, reaction |
| Insert | Cut to detail | Object, information |
| Cross-Cut / Parallel | Between two scenes | Tension, comparison |
| Smash Cut | Abrupt cut | Shock, contrast |
| J-Cut | Audio leads picture | Smooth, immersive |
| L-Cut | Audio trails picture | Overlap, transition |
| Whip Pan | Fast pan transition | Energy, comedy |
| Flash Cut | Very brief cut | Memory, impact |
| Fade | To/from black | End, transition |
| Dissolve | Blend two shots | Time passage |
| Wipe | Geometric transition | Stylized, retro |

## HOLLYWOOD "INVISIBLE CUT" RULES
1. Cut on action (movement masks the edit)
2. Cut on blinks (natural perception boundary)
3. Cut on sound (audio cue hides the cut)
4. Cut on matching composition (visual continuity)
5. Cut on matching eyeline (spatial continuity)
6. Cut on emotional beat (beat = cut)

## 180° RULE (Never Break)
- Establish an axis of action
- Keep camera on one side
- Never cross the line (disorients audience)
- Exception: deliberate disorientation (Fincher, Aronofsky)

## 30° RULE
- Cut to a shot at least 30° different in angle
- Otherwise looks like a jump cut
- Standard coverage exceeds 30° (master vs OTS vs single)

## MATCHING RULES
- Eyeline match — characters look in same direction
- Match on action — movement continues across cut
- Match on composition — subject in similar frame position
- Match on sound — audio bridge
- Match on color/tone — avoid jarring contrast

## RHYTHM & PACING

### Pace by Genre
| Genre | Average Shot Length (ASL) |
|---|---|
| Action (Bourne, Wick) | 1.5–2.5 sec |
| Thriller | 2.5–4 sec |
| Drama | 4–6 sec |
| Comedy | 3–5 sec (setup + punchline) |
| Documentary | 5–8 sec |
| Art house (Malick) | 8–15+ sec |
| Classical Hollywood (Hitchcock) | 6–10 sec |

### Rhythm Tools
- Cut on beat (music-driven)
- Vary ASL by scene (tension = shorter)
- Longer shots = contemplation
- Shorter shots = urgency
- Hold on reaction = emotion
- Cut away = information

### Tension Building (Editing)
1. Establish threat (wide shot)
2. Cut closer (medium)
3. Cut closer (close-up)
4. Cut to detail (insert)
5. Cut to reaction (cutaway)
6. Cut faster (shorten ASL)
7. Hold on silence (breath)
8. Release (action or cut away)

### Comedy Editing
- The "hold" — stay on actor 1 second longer
- The "button" — cut immediately after punchline
- The "reaction" — cut to other actor's response
- The "callback" — echo earlier setup
- The "rule of 3" — three beats, third subverts

## SCENE CONSTRUCTION

### Building a Scene
1. Identify the scene's purpose (what changes?)
2. Identify entry point (start as late as possible)
3. Identify exit point (leave as early as possible)
4. Cut for performance (best take)
5. Cut for emotion (best reaction)
6. Cut for rhythm (best pace)
7. Cut for clarity (audience understands)
8. Watch in context (scene must serve film)

### Scene Trimming Rules
- Cut the head (enter late)
- Cut the tail (leave early)
- Cut on action, not on dialogue end
- Trim "um," "uh," dead air
- Cut to reaction when info is stated
- Overlap audio (J-cut) for smoothness

### Coverage Strategy
- Master shot — establishes geography
- OTS (2x) — dialogue standard
- Singles (2x) — emotional flexibility
- Inserts — objects, hands, details
- Cutaways — reactions, environment
- Coverage = options in edit

## WORKING WITH TEMP

### Temp Music
- Use during rough cut only
- NEVER final — replace with score
- Watch for "temp love" (director attached to temp)
- Communicate with composer EARLY
- Delete temp from final mix

### Temp VFX
- Simple approximations (slashes, composites)
- Mark "TEMP" in bin name
- VFX editor tracks all temps
- Turnover to VFX house at lock

### Temp Sound
- Basic effects to sell the cut
- Dialogue cleaned (basic)
- Music spots marked
- Full sound team takes over at lock

## VFX TURNOVER (Critical Skill)

### VFX Shot List (per shot)
- Scene / shot / take
- Timecode in / out
- Frame count
- Description (what's needed)
- Reference (concept art, previz)
- Handles (extra frames before/after for VFX)
- Priority (hero / standard / background)

### Turnover Workflow
1. Editor exports VFX shot list at picture lock
2. VFX editor creates EDL / AAF per shot
3. VFX supervisor reviews and approves
4. VFX house receives plates + specs
5. VFX house delivers shots (with handles)
6. Editor conforms VFX back into timeline
7. Each VFX version tracked in bin (v001, v002…)

### VFX Handles
- Standard: 12–24 frames before/after
- Complex (comp): 48+ frames
- Reason: allow VFX to blend edges

## SOUND & MUSIC TURNOVER

### Sound Turnover (to sound team)
- AAF / OMF export at picture lock
- Include all dialogue, temp sound, temp music
- Mark all temp FX and music
- Include spotting notes (where music starts/stops)
- Include alt takes if director wants

### Music Turnover (to composer)
- Spotting session (director + editor + composer)
- Temp track (what music should do)
- List of music cues (start/end timecode)
- Scene descriptions (emotional intent)
- Reference tracks (2–3 examples)
- After lock: composer writes original score

### Music Editor (separate role)
- Cuts temp music for rough cut
- Spots music cues
- Prepares music for composer
- Edits final score to picture
- Delivers music stems to mix

## CONTINUITY & EDIT LOGIC

### Continuity Errors (avoid)
- Eyeline direction (looking wrong way)
- Prop continuity (glass empty vs full)
- Costume continuity (jacket on vs off)
- Hair continuity (different style)
- Weather continuity (rain vs sun)
- Actor position (moved between takes)
- Light direction (shadows change)
- Time of day (day vs night)

### Script Supervisor Notes
- Editor receives scripty notes per take
- Marks circle takes (best performance)
- Notes continuity per take
- Notes eyelines
- Notes props

### Editorial Response
- If continuity broken: cut away, insert, or use other take
- If geography unclear: add establishing shot
- If timeline confusing: add insert (clock, phone)
- If emotion weak: use reaction shot

## EDITORIAL DIAGNOSTICS

### "Scene feels flat"
-> Check: stakes, conflict, objective, entrance/exit, pacing, ASL, music

### "Scene feels slow"
-> Trim head/tail, remove redundant lines, cut to reaction, shorten ASL

### "Film feels long"
-> ASL, redundant scenes, subplot resolve, character arc trim, test screening

### "Film feels confusing"
-> Add establishing, fix eyelines, insert information, clarity cut

### "Film feels emotionally cold"
-> Hold on faces, longer ASL, music support, remove exposition

### "Film feels amateur"
-> Inconsistent ASL, weak match cuts, poor coverage, temp love, no rhythm

### "Film feels rushed"
-> Reduce ASL, more breathing room, hold on reaction, allow silence

### "Film feels indulgent"
-> ASL too long, self-aware shots, no trims, poor coverage

## EDITOR-DIRECTOR COLLABORATION

### Best Practices
- Director gives vision, editor executes
- Editor proposes, director decides
- Weekly or daily screenings
- Notes in writing (avoid "make it better")
- Trust each other's instincts
- Silence in screening room (let cut breathe)
- Test audience data informs, not dictates
- Final cut is director's (unless contract says otherwise)

### Note-Taking
- Specific ("Trim 6 frames at 01:23:45")
- Emotional ("Scene needs more tension")
- Structural ("Subplot B resolves too early")
- Never "Make it better" or "I'll know it when I see it"
